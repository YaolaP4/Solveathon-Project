How it works

The system ranks and explains. Staff decide. Here is exactly how a score is made, what the AI does and does not do, and what the tool cannot see.

Every morning (about 6 AM Eastern)

  1. Pull every posted and forecasted opportunity from the Grants.gov public API (about 1,500), plus the full listing for each. The untouched responses are kept for audit.
  2. Clean: parse dates and amounts, drop closed, archived and administrative notices, remove duplicates, and flag incomplete listings instead of hiding them.
  3. Score every opportunity against the 20 objectives of the NC DHHS Strategic Plan with the formula below.
  4. Explain: for the top 40 agency-eligible matches and the top 5 research matches, an AI model writes a summary and a reason, with quotes that our code checks against the listing.
  5. Publish the ranked list, alerts (new since yesterday, closing within 10 days) and a run log. Staff record Pursue, Maybe or Pass.

Confidence: High at {{ doc.method.bands.high if doc else 60 }} or more, Medium at {{ doc.method.bands.medium if doc else 42 }} or more, otherwise Low. Low matches are hidden by default but one click away.

NIH-style research grants (R01, U01 and similar) go to a separate “research” list: NC DHHS would join them as a partner of a university, not lead them.

What the AI does, and the checks on it

  • Writes a 2–3 sentence summary, why it matches the plan objective, who can apply, the deadline, and the biggest risk. It is told to write “not stated” instead of guessing.
  • Must quote the listing word for word. Our code checks every quote; a quote that is not found is hidden and logged on the Evaluation page.
  • It never sets the score. It can only move a record to “not eligible” when it quotes eligibility text that proves it, or lower the confidence one step when it says the match is weak. Both are shown on the record.
  • Runs on Azure OpenAI inside the team's Azure subscription. Only public Grants.gov text and the public strategic plan are sent to it.

Known limitations

  • The plan covers 2023–2025; NC DHHS priorities may have moved since.
  • Grants.gov only. State pass-through funds, foundations and SAM.gov contract notices are not included.
  • A short or vague listing can score low even when it fits (a false negative). Low matches are kept, one click away.
  • Eligibility is read from structured fields and text; edge cases (for example “other, see text”) need a human check.
  • Prototype: no sign-in yet. A pilot would use Entra ID sign-in so decisions are tied to real accounts.
